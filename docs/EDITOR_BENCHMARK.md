# Editorial workflow benchmark

An agency editor should be able to answer three questions before rendering a candidate. What promise does the opening make? Where does the source deliver on it? What will the chosen crop or caption treatment obscure? Several products give editors a chance to answer these questions before generating their final cuts.

## Evidence

Official product and help pages were read on November 19, 2026. The comparisons below describe documented controls. No client footage was uploaded and no vendor renders, accounts or paid APIs were used. Comparative output quality, task completion time, bilingual accuracy and audience response remain unmeasured.

## Applying the benchmark to the first pilot

1. **Selection.** Before seeing the ranking, have the reviewing editor mark standalone moments in authorized footage. For each shortlisted candidate, record the opening, payoff, missing context and any overlap with another candidate. Count how many of the first ten the editor keeps and how many distinct marked moments appear in the shortlist.
2. **Captions and framing.** Check representative English, Egyptian Arabic and code-switched speech against a human transcript. Record word substitutions, omissions and insertions with the reference word count for each language sample. At speaker switches, slides and gestures, review the composed frame with platform overlays. Include a caption containing Arabic, a Latin brand name and a number. Record readability and speaker attribution separately from spelling.
3. **Editor effort.** Time the steps from opening a candidate through correcting one caption, adjusting a cut, comparing the rendered result and recording a decision. Capture editor time and render time separately. Keep the observations for rejected candidates as well as approved ones.
4. **Delivery.** Review the beginning and ending at normal speed with audio, check caption placement and verify the revision under review. Record approval for that revision and client. Any claims about views or retention require measured distribution data from an authorized channel.
